\section{Prompt-Component Study}
\label{sec:prompts}
% NEW (written for the TOMPECS version; not in the original paper)
This section reports the benchmark-scale ablation behind RQ1: six prompt variants, two models and
six HeCBench kernels.

\subsection{Prompt Variants}
\label{sec:ablation}
The full prompt is not a monolith: it bundles a persona, hardware-specific guardrails, a set of
prohibitions, a formal-equivalence requirement, a request for an explanation of each change, and an
output-format specification. To find out which of these actually matter, we evaluate six variants, each
of which removes or adds exactly one component while leaving the source code and the profiling data
in place. Two further variants (\texttt{no\_format}, \texttt{no\_hardware}) are implemented but excluded
from the measured grid, for the reasons noted below.

\paragraph{No Proof (\texttt{no\_proof}).}
The full prompt requires the model to state preconditions, postconditions and loop invariants, and to
argue that the optimized kernel is semantically equivalent to the original. Removing this requirement
lets the model apply a transformation without having to justify it. The consequence is visible on
\texttt{shmembench}, whose swap loop preserves an algebraic sum invariant: without the proof
requirement, Gemini~2.5~Pro exploits that invariant to eliminate shared-memory traffic altogether and
reports a $213\times$ apparent speedup that reflects no hardware improvement. GPT-5 applies the same
transformation under every variant, including the full prompt---which is what motivates the explicit
semantic-preservation constraint of Section~\ref{sec:intent}.

\paragraph{No Constraints (\texttt{no\_constraints}).}
The full prompt carries a numbered list of prohibitions: do not rename or remove the entry point, do
not change kernel signatures, do not introduce new top-level symbols, do not trade correctness for
speed. This variant deletes every such line, giving the model unconstrained freedom to restructure.
The effect is model-dependent rather than uniformly good or bad: it produces GPT-5's best
\texttt{bitonic-sort} result ($1.14\times$) and one of Gemini's worst ($0.90\times$).

\paragraph{Minimal (\texttt{minimal}).}
This variant replaces the entire instruction block---persona, guardrails, prohibitions, proof
requirement, explanation requirement and format specification---with a single sentence:
\textit{``Optimize the following CUDA code for execution time.''} The source code and the profiling
summaries are still supplied, so \texttt{minimal} isolates the value of the \emph{instructions} rather
than of the evidence; removing the evidence instead is the separate ablation of
Section~\ref{sec:profabl}. \texttt{Minimal} is the only variant that degrades both models: on
\texttt{md5hash} GPT-5 produces code $51\%$ slower than its best variant, and on \texttt{shmembench}
Gemini produces code roughly four times slower than under any other variant.

\paragraph{No Explanation (\texttt{no\_explanation}).}
The full prompt asks the model to return, alongside the code, a structured list of the optimizations it
applied and those it considered and rejected. Dropping these blocks removes a form of implicit
chain-of-thought: having to name and justify each change encourages the model to commit only to changes
it can defend. Its measured effect is small and inconsistent---it is Gemini's best
\texttt{bitonic-sort} variant ($1.13\times$) and has no discernible effect elsewhere---but it also
removes the record from which we reconstruct \emph{why} a run behaved as it did.

\paragraph{No Role (\texttt{no\_role}).}
The full prompt opens by casting the model as an HPC performance-optimization expert targeting a
specific GPU, and follows with an architecture-specific guidance block. This variant removes both.
It is the variant most likely to produce a pathological run rather than a merely worse one: GPT-5 under
\texttt{no\_role} is the single timeout in the grid (\texttt{accuracy}), while Gemini completes the same
configuration without incident.

\paragraph{Chain-of-Thought (\texttt{cot}).}
Unlike the others, \texttt{cot} adds a component rather than removing one: a single instruction,
\textit{``Think step by step about which bottlenecks to address before writing any code,''} is
prepended to the instruction block. It is the only variant that never degrades either model relative to
the full prompt, reaching the best observed speedup for both on \texttt{bitonic-sort}
($1.14\times$ and $1.13\times$). It is not uniformly safe, however: Gemini's \texttt{sobol} run under
\texttt{cot} introduced \texttt{\_\_builtin\_ctz}---a host-only intrinsic---inside a
\texttt{\_\_global\_\_} kernel and could not repair the resulting compile error within three iterations.

\paragraph{Variants not evaluated.}
\texttt{no\_format} strips the output-format specification and asks only for optimized code. We exclude
it because it perturbs the reliability of automated code extraction rather than the quality of the
optimization, which would confound the metric we are measuring rather than inform it.
\texttt{no\_hardware} removes the architecture-specific guidance block and the target-architecture line;
its effect is largely subsumed by \texttt{no\_role}, which removes the same guidance block together with
the persona.

\subsection{Results}
\label{sec:results}
The ablation study evaluated GPT-5 and Gemini 2.5 Pro across six prompt variants and six HeCBench benchmarks, yielding 72 experimental runs in total (2 models × 6 variants × 6 apps). Across all successful runs, GPT-5 demonstrated consistently higher first-attempt success rates, requiring only a single build iteration in 32 of 36 cases compared to Gemini's 24 of 36. Gemini exhausted all three repair iterations on 8 runs (4 failures, 4 eventually repaired), with sobol being its most problematic benchmark (3 outright failures due to correctness violations and a persistent \texttt{\_\_builtin\_ctz} host-device mismatch).

\subsection{Findings}
\label{sec:analysis}
\paragraph{Optimization quality.}
Within the ablation configuration (\texttt{pc+rl} input, one prompt component ablated at a time),
neither model delivers large or reliable speedups. The best sustained result across all valid runs is
$1.14\times$ (GPT-5 on \texttt{bitonic-sort} under \texttt{no\_constraints}, \texttt{cot} or
\texttt{no\_proof}) and $1.13\times$ (Gemini on the same benchmark under \texttt{no\_explanation} or
\texttt{cot}). On \texttt{sobol} both models either fail or match the baseline within $2\%$. On
\texttt{atan2}, \texttt{md5hash} and \texttt{accuracy} the runtime distributions are dominated by
variant effects rather than by any genuine optimization.

\paragraph{Prompt components are not equally important.}
The \texttt{minimal} variant consistently underperforms---most clearly on \texttt{md5hash}
(GPT-5: $396{,}800$\,\textmu s versus $262{,}500$\,\textmu s under \texttt{no\_constraints}, about
$1.5\times$ slower) and on \texttt{shmembench} (Gemini: $18{,}735$\,\textmu s versus roughly
$4{,}660$\,\textmu s for every other variant). Stripping contextual framing measurably degrades
optimization quality. Conversely, \texttt{cot} is stable or slightly better for both models, though the
improvement is modest. Every other component is model-dependent: removing constraints helps GPT-5 on
\texttt{bitonic-sort} but hurts Gemini; removing the explanation helps Gemini on the same benchmark but
has no consistent effect elsewhere; and removing the role assignment caused a timeout for GPT-5 on
\texttt{accuracy} while Gemini handled it without issue. \texttt{Minimal} is the only variant that
consistently degrades both models, and \texttt{cot} the only one that never hurts either.

\paragraph{Reliability.}
GPT-5 succeeds on the first attempt in $32$ of $36$ runs, Gemini~2.5~Pro in $24$ of $36$. Gemini
exhausts all three repair iterations on eight runs, four of which are eventually repaired and four of
which fail outright; \texttt{sobol} accounts for three of the four failures, through correctness
violations and the host-only-intrinsic error described in Section~\ref{sec:ablation}. First-attempt
success rate, not peak speedup, is the dimension on which the two models differ most.
